Why margin needs to be live, not retrospective

In a project business the product is time, and time cannot be recovered once it has been spent on the wrong thing. A profitability report produced after delivery describes a decision that can no longer be made.

Tracking effort against the estimate as the work happens changes the conversation. A project at seventy per cent of budget and forty per cent of scope is a discussion with the client this week, not a write-off next quarter.

We configure the project structure around how you actually sell — retainers, fixed scope, time and materials, or a mix — because a structure that does not match your contracts produces numbers your own managers quietly ignore.

FAQ

Questions we are asked before signing

Will our team actually fill in timesheets?

That depends more on friction than on policy. We configure entry to take seconds — a week view, sensible defaults, mobile entry — and set the approval rhythm so that it is a weekly habit rather than a monthly reconstruction.

Can we run retainers and fixed-price work together?

Yes. Billing method is set per project, so retainers, fixed scope and time and materials all run in the same system and report into the same profitability view.

Does it show utilisation by person?

Yes, as billable hours against available hours, by person, team and period. Combined with committed project work, this is what makes the forward capacity view usable.

Can clients see progress?

A client portal can expose project status, approved documents and invoices where that suits your relationship. What is shared is configurable, and most firms start conservatively.
