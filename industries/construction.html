Why contracting needs cost tracking in the ERP

Construction is unusual in that the commitment is made long before the cost is recorded. A purchase order placed today becomes an invoice next month and a payment the month after, and a system that only sees the invoice is always reporting the past.

Running projects in the ERP makes committed cost visible at the point of ordering. A site manager who has issued eighty per cent of the budgeted steel with half the structure up is a problem you can still act on; the same fact discovered at final accounts is only a lesson.

We model each site as its own warehouse so material does not vanish from the books when it leaves the central store, and configure subcontractor certification against recorded measurement so that the monthly bill conversation starts from shared data.

FAQ

Questions we are asked before signing

Can we track material issued to each site separately?

Yes. Each site is set up as a warehouse, so transfers, consumption and the balance lying at site are all visible. Reconciling the site balance becomes a routine check rather than a year-end write-off.

Does it handle retention and subcontractor certification?

Yes. Retention is held against the subcontractor invoice and released on your terms, and certification is recorded against measurement so the amount payable is derived rather than negotiated from scratch.

Can we bill on percentage completion?

Yes, along with milestone billing and measurement-based billing. Most contractors use more than one, so we configure the method per contract type rather than site-wide.

What about equipment and plant?

Plant is handled as assets with maintenance schedules, and hire charges can be allocated to the project using it so that project cost includes the equipment it consumed.
