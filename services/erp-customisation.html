When customisation is the right answer

The honest default is to use ERPNext as it ships. Standard features get upgraded, tested and documented by a large community; custom code becomes your responsibility forever. We push hard on configuration before we write anything.

But some gaps are real. A pricing model nobody else uses, an approval chain tied to your licence conditions, a report your regulator wants in a specific shape — these are worth building, and the Frappe framework is built for exactly that.

We deliver customisation as a separate versioned app, never as edits to the core. That keeps your ERPNext upgradeable: when a new version lands, your custom app moves with it instead of blocking the upgrade for three years.

FAQ

Questions we are asked before signing

Will customisation stop us upgrading ERPNext?

Not if it is done properly. We ship customisation as a separate app that hooks into core rather than editing it, which is what keeps upgrades possible. Direct core edits are the reason many ERP installations are frozen on an old version.

Do we own the code you write?

Yes. Custom work is delivered to your repository and your site. ERPNext and Frappe themselves are GNU GPLv3, and anything we build for you is yours to keep, read and change.

Can you customise an ERPNext someone else implemented?

Yes, and we do it often. We start with a short review of the existing configuration and custom code, because what looks like a missing feature is sometimes a misconfiguration that is cheaper to correct.

How do you price customisation?

Small, well-specified items are fixed price. Longer programmes of work run on a monthly retainer with an agreed backlog, which suits businesses that keep finding improvements after go-live.
