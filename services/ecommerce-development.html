The problem with a disconnected store

A store running on its own database needs a sync. The sync runs on a schedule, the schedule has gaps, and in those gaps you oversell items you do not have and hold orders you could have shipped. Somebody then keys the orders into the ERP by hand, and the stock figures drift further apart.

When the storefront reads from the same system that runs your warehouse, that whole class of problem disappears. Stock shown is stock available. A price rule set for a customer group applies online without being re-entered. An order placed at midnight is in the warehouse queue at opening.

We build both customer-facing stores and B2B portals where logged-in customers see their own contracted pricing, credit position and order history — which for most distributors turns out to be worth considerably more than a public checkout.

FAQ

Questions we are asked before signing

Can you work with our existing Shopify or WooCommerce store?

Yes. Where you want to keep the storefront you have, we build the integration layer so that stock, pricing, orders and invoices move correctly between it and ERPNext, with proper handling of failures rather than a silent nightly job.

Does this handle B2B pricing and credit limits?

Yes, and it is the part most distributors care about. Logged-in customers see their contracted price list, their credit position and their own order history, all read from the ERP at the moment the page loads.

Which payment gateways do you support?

The common Indian and international gateways integrate cleanly. We confirm the specific gateway during scoping, since settlement timing and reconciliation behaviour differ and both affect your accounts.

Who maintains the store after launch?

Usually us, under the same annual contract as the ERP. The store is part of the same system, and splitting support between two vendors is how small integration faults turn into long arguments.
