Why project costing belongs in the ERP

A dedicated project tool tracks tasks well and costs badly. It does not know what the material cost, what the subcontractor invoiced, or what was billed — those live in the accounting system, which in turn knows nothing about progress.

Running projects where the money already lives closes that gap. Timesheets, purchase invoices, material issues and sales invoices all post against the project, so margin is a live figure rather than a year-end discovery.

We configure ERPNext Projects around how your business quotes and bills: milestone billing, percentage completion, time and materials, or retainers. The structure matters, because a project hierarchy that does not match your contracts produces reports nobody trusts.

FAQ

Questions we are asked before signing

Can we keep using our current task tool?

You can, and some teams do for day-to-day task management while costing runs in ERPNext. Be aware that two systems means two places to update status, which is usually abandoned within a few months.

Does it handle percentage-of-completion billing?

Yes, along with milestone billing, time and materials, and retainers. We configure the method per project type, because most contracting businesses use more than one.

How do subcontractor costs get captured?

Through purchase orders and invoices raised against the project. Committed cost appears as soon as the order is placed, which is what makes the forecast useful rather than merely accurate after the fact.

Can staff enter time on their phones?

Yes. Timesheet entry works on mobile, and for field-heavy teams we often build a stripped-down app so that entering a day takes seconds rather than opening the full desk interface.
