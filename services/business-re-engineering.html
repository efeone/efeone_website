Why the process comes before the software

Almost every ERP project that disappoints people was configured to match a process nobody had examined. The approvals that exist because of one incident in 2014, the duplicate register kept because somebody once lost a file, the report assembled by hand every Friday — these get encoded into the new system and preserved for another decade.

A re-engineering engagement examines the process as it is actually performed, not as the manual describes it. We follow real transactions end to end, sit with the people doing the work, and measure where time and accuracy are lost.

The output is a set of process maps, a list of changes with the effort and benefit of each, and a recommendation on what the ERP should automate, what should simply stop, and what should stay manual because automating it is not worth the cost.

FAQ

Questions we are asked before signing

Do we have to buy an ERP implementation afterwards?

No. This is a standalone engagement with its own deliverable. Some clients run it to prepare for an ERP, some to prepare for an audit, and some because they want to know where the time goes before deciding anything.

How disruptive is it for our team?

Expect a few hours per department over the engagement, mostly in short sessions at the desk. We work around production schedules rather than pulling people into a meeting room for a day.

Will you recommend cutting roles?

That is not what we are hired for, and we do not frame findings that way. We identify work that does not need doing; what you then do with the capacity that frees up is a decision for you.

What if our processes are undocumented?

That is the normal starting point, and it is why the engagement is built around observation rather than document review. If you already had accurate documentation you would not need the mapping.
