What an ERPNext implementation involves

Most businesses do not need more software. They need the software they already have to stop disagreeing with itself — stock that matches the ledger, invoices that match what was delivered, and a month-end close that does not depend on one person’s spreadsheet.

ERPNext covers accounting, stock, manufacturing, sales, purchase, projects, HR and CRM in one open-source system. There are no per-user licence fees, and because the source is open, anything genuinely specific to your business can be built rather than worked around.

The work that decides whether an implementation succeeds happens before any configuration: understanding how your business actually runs, deciding what should change, and agreeing what the system must produce. That is where we start, and it is why our scope documents are written before a single module is switched on.

FAQ

Questions we are asked before signing

How long does an ERPNext implementation take?

For a single-company business with standard processes, eight to sixteen weeks from discovery to go-live. Multi-company groups, heavy manufacturing and bespoke apps extend that, and we say so in the scope rather than at month four.

What does ERPNext cost?

The software itself is open source, so there are no per-user licence fees. You pay for hosting and for implementation work. That is usually why businesses compare it against licensed products in the first place.

Can ERPNext handle Indian GST compliance?

Yes. GST invoicing, e-invoicing, e-way bills and the GST returns are supported through the India Compliance app, which we configure as part of the implementation.

Do you migrate data from our current system?

Yes — masters, open transactions and opening balances. We reconcile the migrated figures against your old system and give you the reconciliation, so nobody has to take it on trust.

What happens after go-live?

An annual maintenance contract covers version upgrades, bug fixes, new reports and user support. Most clients also keep a small budget for the improvements they think of once the system is in daily use.
