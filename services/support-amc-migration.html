What support actually has to cover

Three things change after go-live. ERPNext releases new versions; your business changes how it works; and the people who were trained at launch leave and are replaced. A support contract that only fixes bugs addresses one third of the problem.

Our annual contract covers version upgrades on a planned schedule, defect fixes against an agreed response time, new reports and small enhancements from a backlog you prioritise, and user support for the people doing the work.

We also take over systems implemented by someone else. That begins with a review of the configuration and custom code, a written assessment of what is sound and what is not, and an upgrade path — which for a long-frozen installation is usually the first thing anyone has written down about it.

FAQ

Questions we are asked before signing

We are on an old ERPNext version. Can it be upgraded?

Almost always, though the path depends on how much of the core was edited directly. The assessment tells you the real cost before you commit, and a staged upgrade through intermediate versions is often safer than one jump.

Will you support a system another vendor built?

Yes. We start with a written assessment rather than a promise, because taking on an unexamined system helps nobody. If we think the honest answer is a rebuild, we will tell you that.

What response times do you offer?

They are set in the agreement and depend on what the system does for you. A production-stopping fault in a plant running shifts needs a different commitment from a report formatting issue, and the contract should say so explicitly.

Are new features included or charged separately?

The contract includes a monthly allowance for enhancements, which covers most ongoing requests. Larger pieces of work are scoped and quoted separately so that neither side is guessing.
